The Topic

De-Googling & Big Tech Data Collection

Every app you open, every search you run, and every website you visit leaves a trail. This page explains who is collecting that data, how they use it, and what you can do to stop it.

Section 1

What is Big Tech Data Collection?

Data collection is the process of gathering information about your habits, preferences, location, contacts, and behaviour; through the apps and services you use. Companies like Google, Meta (Facebook), Microsoft, and Amazon have built entire business models around collecting this data and selling targeted advertising based on it.

This isn't just a conspiracy theory. It is publicly documented in the terms of service that almost nobody reads. When you agree to use Gmail, Google Search, Google Maps, or YouTube, you are granting Google permission to collect and process enormous amounts of personal information.

Did you know? Google processes over 8.5 billion searches per day. Every single one of those searches, when made while logged in or with cookies enabled is tied to a profile built around you, including your age, location, interests, income bracket, and purchasing habits.

What kinds of data are collected?

  • Location Data

    Your GPS coordinates, home and work address, places you visit regularly, and your travel patterns; even when location is "turned off" on some services.

  • Search & Browse History

    Every query you search, every page you visit, and how long you spend on each site is logged and analysed to build an interest profile.

  • Communications

    Email content, contact lists, and message metadata are scanned. Gmail has famously scanned email content to serve personalised ads for years.

  • Device & App Usage

    What apps you use, when you use them, what you buy, and how you interact with your device, this all sent back to servers and stored indefinitely.

  • Voice & Audio

    Smart assistants like Google Assistant and Alexa record audio clips which are reviewed by human contractors to improve voice recognition.

  • Financial Behaviour

    Purchase history, price comparisons, and financial searches are tracked to build a detailed picture of your economic situation.

Section 2

Why Your Privacy Matters

A common response to privacy concerns is: "I have nothing to hide." But privacy isn't about hiding wrongdoing it's about autonomy, dignity, and control over your own life. You wouldn't hand a stranger your diary, your bank statements, your bank details, and your location history. So why hand it all to a corporation?

The real-world consequences

Data collected today doesn't disappear. It is stored, bought, sold, leaked, and sometimes handed to governments. The consequences of mass surveillance and data harvesting are already visible in the world:

Targeted manipulation: The Cambridge Analytica scandal revealed how data that was harvested from 87 million Facebook profiles was used to build psychological profiles and deliver targeted political advertising designed to influence elections in multiple countries including the United States and the United Kingdom.

The chilling effect

When people know they are being watched, they change their behaviour. They self-censor. They avoid searching for topics that might look suspicious. They conform. This is called the chilling effect, and it is a documented consequence of mass surveillance that threatens free thought and free expression, even if you personally never do anything wrong.

Data breaches are inevitable

No company has a perfect security record. The more data that is stored about you, the more damage a breach can cause. Major breaches in recent years have exposed the personal information of billions of people; including names, addresses, passwords, and financial details to corporations around the world.

Section 3

Privacy-Respecting Alternatives

De-Googling doesn't have to happen overnight. Every single swap you make, even just changing your search engine can reduce the amount of data being collected about you. Here are the most impactful replacements, starting with the easiest.

Search Engines

  • DuckDuckGo

    The most popular private search engine. Does not track searches, build profiles, or serve targeted ads. Easy drop-in replacement for Google Search.

  • Brave Search

    Independent search index not relying on Google or Bing. Built by the same team behind the Brave browser. Strong privacy defaults out of the box.

Web Browsers

  • Firefox

    Open-source browser from Mozilla. Highly configurable with strong privacy extensions available. A solid everyday choice.

  • LibreWolf

    A hardened Firefox fork with privacy settings pre-configured out of the box. Strips telemetry and comes with uBlock Origin included by default.

  • Brave Browser

    Chromium-based browser with built-in ad blocking, tracker blocking, and fingerprinting protection. Fast and beginner-friendly.

Email

  • ProtonMail

    End-to-end encrypted email hosted in Switzerland. Free tier available. Even Proton themselves cannot read your emails.

  • Tutanota

    Open-source encrypted email service based in Germany. Strong privacy laws apply under German and EU legislation.
